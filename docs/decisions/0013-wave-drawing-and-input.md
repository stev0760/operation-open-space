# ADR 0013: Wave drawing and input

- **Status:** Accepted. [DESIGN]
- **Scope:** how the port draws the wave (the play area, the sprites, the HUD and its child windows) on the surface of ADR 0006, and how the wave's polled keys reach the flight model under the frame model of ADR 0001. The drawing routines and the input sites themselves are in [world.md](../re/world.md); this ADR decides only what the port changes and what it keeps bit for bit. [DESIGN]

## Context
- **The original draws straight to the window.** All wave drawing goes to the window DC; memory DCs are only blit sources. A sprite erases itself: the sprite routine blacks the strips its last cell uncovered, or the whole old cell, and blits the new cell, and the player's pinned ship skips the erase ([Drawing](../re/world.md#drawing)). Nothing clears the play area between frames; the stars, the gates and the border persist on the window from whenever they were drawn. [BYTES seg11:0xb33 seg8:0xb76 seg7:0x1208]
- **A repaint rebuilds the HUD.** The repaint routine lays out the HUD strip from the client width, destroys and recreates the scrollbar and the two `IS_INFO` child windows, fills the strip, draws the circuit-board border, the bevel and the panels, and then redraws the state's screen ([Play-area frame and status display](../re/world.md#play-area-frame-and-status-display), [Child windows](../re/world.md#child-windows)). It runs through the flag DGROUP:0x8f64 from the main window's paint path, and the oracle shows 18 child creations in wave 1 for 6 repaints. [BYTES seg11:0xafe seg11:0xc5d seg11:0xcd9 seg11:0xd4e seg11:0xd9f seg2:0x970] [ORACLE wave-0001]
- **ADR 0006 already moved the window.** The port's game draws into an 8-bit index surface of the logical size, presented by an integer factor; its DC stands for the original's window DC ([ADR 0006](0006-window-scaling.md) decision 2, [ADR 0007](0007-palette.md)). A system-drawn child window on top of that surface is drawn at device pixels, outside the factor. [DESIGN]
- **The flight keys are polled, not messaged.** The arrows, Ctrl, Shift and Space have no case in the key-message switch. Turning is read on frames with `GetAsyncKeyState` bit 0x8000; thrust and brake (Up and Down) with mask 0x8001, so a tap shorter than the polling interval still acts once through the "pressed since the last call" bit; fire is read on every pass with 0x8000 only ([Polled keys](../re/world.md#polled-keys)). [BYTES seg11:0x5978 seg11:0x5f81 seg11:0x5fb9 seg11:0x4db0]
- **The original polls far faster than it frames.** Its busy loop runs about 11 500 passes per second in the wave, so the fire poll sees any tap; the port runs one pass before each frame and after each batch of messages, about 36 per second ([ADR 0001](0001-timestep.md) decision 5 and consequences). [BYTES seg7:0x881] [ORACLE newgame-0002] [DESIGN]
- **Frame-counted arithmetic is 16-bit.** The ship update counts a 0 … 8 cycle in DGROUP:0x95f6 and runs thrust, brake and drag on its even values; the integrator, the view, the sprite source offsets and the scaling products are 16-bit, and the oracle's positions depend on their truncation ([Ships](../re/world.md#ships-seg130x0), [World coordinates](../re/world.md#world-coordinates)). [BYTES seg13:0x18 seg13:0x23b seg8:0x1b54 seg7:0x1208]

## Decision
1. **The surface is the window.** The wave draws on the ADR 0006 surface with the original's calls in the original's order: the same erase strips, the same ROPs, the same pinned-player path, the same icon-dissolve and radar blits. The port never clears the play area between frames and never redraws what the original leaves standing; where the original blacks the screen (the wave set-up, the state changes), the port blacks the surface at the same point. The surface persists across states as the window did. The presenter shows it after every frame and on `WM_PAINT`. In states 1 and 7 a `WM_PAINT` runs the paint routine and then blacks the logical cover of the update region on the surface, as seg2:0x7ac runs seg2:0x970 before `DefWindowProc`, whose `BeginPaint` erases with the class brush `BLACK_BRUSH`. [DESIGN] [BYTES seg8:0xb76 seg7:0x1208 seg7:0x3a42 seg9:0xd6c seg2:0x7c4 seg2:0x7c7 seg6:0x14a8]
2. **No second buffer, no interpolation.** The port keeps no cleared back buffer and no scene list; a frame's pixels are the surface after that frame's draws, so a comparison with the oracle is a comparison of draw sequences. Interpolated or double-buffered rendering is a separate ADR if ever wanted. [DESIGN]
3. **The repaint path stays.** The port keeps DGROUP:0x8f64's role, the repaint routine's order (layout, children, strip, border, bevel, panels, state screen) and its triggers, so a dialog's return redraws the HUD as the oracle shows. A `WM_PAINT` redraws the HUD only through that flag. The surface is erased where the original's window was on the target machine: by the `BeginPaint` erase of decision 1. An activation of main draws nothing ([ADR 0007](0007-palette.md) decision 5): the palette message that erased and repainted the client on a 256-colour display is not sent on the target's display, and the port does not emulate it. Main has no `WS_CLIPCHILDREN`, so the `BeginPaint` erase also invalidates its children: the `IS_INFO` panels paint again after it. [DESIGN] [BYTES seg2:0x970 seg11:0x15b3 seg2:0x5ff seg2:0x607 seg7:0x649b seg6:0x180e] [REF Win32 SDK RedrawWindow]
   - **The full status display from the first frame of the wave.** The original on a real Windows 98 machine shows the full status display during play (the border, the bevel, SCORE, the fuel gauge, the clock, the lamps, the weapon panel) with no dialog having opened ([win98-0001](../../oracle/digests/win98-0001.md)); the Wine oracle's bare display at the wave start, with only the child windows, the radar and the clock (wave-0001 shots 05 to 12), is Wine's palette message at the `IS_ICON` column's reactivation of main (seg7:0x649b), which Wine sends on every activation and the NT model only on palette displays. The Display Options setting (Game Border) does not account for it: wave-0001's shot 13 shows the border after a dialog under the same INI. So the port draws the status display once, through seg11:0x3cc2 at the wave start, and leaves it standing across the column's creation; a `rand()` comparison against the Wine oracle sees one seeding fewer at the wave start than Wine's second seg11:0xafe run makes. The column's reactivation of main also restores the foreground window, because a window manager (Wine on a desktop) gives the new owned window the input focus and the timer's focus test (seg2:0x1327) would otherwise pause the game until the player clicks main. [DESIGN] [ORACLE win98-0001] [ORACLE wave-0001] [MANUAL Display Options] [BYTES seg13:0x66af seg7:0x649b seg11:0x3cc2 seg11:0xafe seg11:0xb22 seg2:0x5cb seg2:0x1327] [REF Wine 11.0 dlls/win32u/input.c] [REF ReactOS win32ss/user/ntuser/focus.c]
4. **`IS_INFO` panels are drawn into the surface.** The two `IS_INFO` windows become port-owned panels with the original's rectangles, modes and paint logic, drawn on the surface so they scale with the factor; a `PostMessage(WM_PAINT)` to one of them becomes a dirty mark that the next frame or repaint honours. The per-repaint destroy-and-create becomes a reset of whatever state the original's window procedure kept per window; the mode words that live in DGROUP are untouched by it, as they were. [DESIGN] [BYTES seg11:0xd4e seg11:0xd9f] [REF ADR 0006 decision 5]
5. **The scrollbar is a real control.** The HUD scrollbar is a Win32 `SCROLLBAR` child created once when the HUD is first laid out in the wave states, moved on relayout, destroyed when the wave states end, and never when the race flag suppresses it. Its rectangle is the original's 14 × 85 at the layout position, multiplied by the factor; its range, position and handler keep the original's inverted zoom. Each repaint sets the range and position as the original's creation does, so a persistent control behaves as a recreated one. The presenter does not paint over it. [DESIGN] [BYTES seg11:0xca7 seg11:0xceb seg11:0x7798]
6. **An input latch replaces direct `GetAsyncKeyState`.** The port keeps a key latch fed by `WM_KEYDOWN` and `WM_KEYUP` on the main window, with two reads per key: `down`, the current state (bit 0x8000), and `pressed`, set on a non-repeat key-down and cleared by the read (bit 0x0001, with the original's "shared across callers" rule kept per key: the first reader clears it). Every polled site reads the latch with the mask the original passes: turning `down` only, Up and Down `down || pressed`. The fire poll reads `down || pressed`, although the original masks 0x8000 only: the original's pass rate let a tap fire, and the port's one pass per frame would miss it. The joystick poll reads `joyGetPos` in place of ordinal 41's game-port count; any failure takes ordinal 41's failure path, a `MessageBeep` and 0xA7 ([ispace.md](../re/ispace.md#ordinal-notes)), so seg11:0x72b2 clears `JoyStick` as it did on a machine without a stick. Mapping the positions onto ordinal 41's counts is [OPEN] ([win16-translation.md](../re/win16-translation.md)). [DESIGN] [BYTES seg11:0x5978 seg11:0x5f81 seg11:0x5fb9 seg11:0x4db0]
7. **The 0 … 8 cycle and the widths stay.** DGROUP:0x95f6's equivalent counts frames exactly as the original does, thrust runs on its even values, and every product, shift and sum that the original computes in 16 bits is computed in 16 bits in the port, with the same signed and unsigned compares, so positions and headings match the oracle frame for frame. The port does not widen them "to fix" wraps. [DESIGN] [BYTES seg13:0x18 seg13:0x23b seg8:0x1b54 seg8:0x29ea]
8. **Draw order follows address order.** Until the draw order of the frame's calls is read ([Open questions](../re/world.md#open-questions)), the port calls them in the order world.md lists for the pass and the frame, and the comparison with `wave-0001` checks the result. [DESIGN] [BYTES seg7:0x58b seg7:0xcd3] [OPEN]
9. **The pass's clip applies to the play-area blits only.** The original selects the window's clip region (DGROUP:0x13dc, DGROUP:0x12) on every pass at seg7:0x84a. The port applies that rectangle to the wave's blits on the window surface (a save, intersect and restore around each call) and not to the HUD's drawing or the radar's blit, which the original draws into the same DC through other paths. The rectangle is seg2:0xca4's: the play area with the Border and PlayMode insets, (68, 78, 956, 663) at 1024 × 768. The briefing entry sets it at its seg2:0xba4 call (seg9:0x5d14), and `WM_SIZE` sets it again. The HUD missing from the oracle's wave-start shots is decision 3's erase, not the clip. [DESIGN] [BYTES seg7:0x842 seg7:0x84a seg2:0xc52 seg2:0xcb8 seg2:0xd54 seg2:0xd69 seg9:0x5d14]
10. **The ship's design comes from the INI's `MainShip`.** The player's flight values are read from the MASTER.SHP record of that name and its MASTER.BOD body through the port's seg14 design path; the probe dump confirms the values, and which reader seg14 uses is [OPEN]. [DESIGN] [ORACLE probe-0001] [OPEN]

## Alternatives
| Alternative | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Clear and redraw the whole play area each frame from object lists | Rejected. It changes what persists on screen (stars, dissolved icons, gates) and makes a pixel comparison with the oracle impossible. [DESIGN] |
| Real `IS_INFO` child windows | Rejected. They would draw at device pixels outside ADR 0006's factor and need their own font scaling. [DESIGN] |
| A port-drawn scrollbar on the surface | Deferred. The original's scrollbar is a system control, drawn by the system in the oracle too, so a real control matches it at factor 1 and looks like the Navigation window's controls at higher factors. [DESIGN] |
| Call `GetAsyncKeyState` directly | Rejected. Its bit 0 is reset by any caller in the process, tests cannot inject it, and the pass rate difference would lose fire taps. The latch reproduces the masks at the original's sites. [DESIGN] [REF Win32 SDK, GetAsyncKeyState] |
| Poll the keys on a thread at the original's pass rate | Rejected. It reintroduces the busy loop ADR 0001 removed for one bit of state. [DESIGN] |

## Consequences
- M3 stage 2 implements decisions 1 to 8 in `src/world/` (pools, movers, flight, view, the latch) and `src/hud/` (the repaint routine, the panels, the `IS_INFO` panels, the scrollbar, the radar), with the sprite routine's erase geometry host-tested against hand-worked cases and the frames compared with `wave-0001`. [DESIGN]
- The main window must clip the scrollbar out of the presenter's blit, or the control repaints under every present. [DESIGN]
- A key held while the window loses focus stays `down` in the latch until its key-up arrives; the original's focus-loss pause covers the same gap. [DESIGN]
- The fire key's `pressed` read is the one place the port acts where the original's bytes would not at the port's pass rate; it is tagged in the source with this ADR. Because the latch is fed in every state, the wave start clears every key's `pressed` bit (`KeyLatch::clear_pressed` from the seg9:0x60bc hook), so a tap during the briefing or a dialog does not fire on the first pass. [DESIGN]
- The 1-bpp image slots (the rock masks, DIGITAL) are index images on the surface's palette; a mono blit sets text index 0 and background 255 for the call, per ADR 0007. [DESIGN]
- The joystick read seg11:0x72b2 goes through ISPACE.DLL ordinal 41 in the original; the port reads `joyGetPos(JOYSTICKID1)` under the same guards (`JoyStick` set, the player's byte +0xe2 zero) and, on any failure, beeps and returns 0xA7 as ordinal 41 does (decision 6). [DESIGN] [BYTES seg11:0x72b2]
- Small port choices without their own ADR, each tagged `[DESIGN]` at its site: the flight globals are copied between `World` and the frame state each frame; the directory records live in memory beside `nav::Records` and a run without an Enter takes `IS_TEST_BRIEFING` or the INI drive root; the ship files are looked for in the key directory, then the asset `SHIPS\` directory, then the asset directory; the DEMO command line's `DScale` draw of seg9:0x237e is skipped; the graphics loads of seg9:0x22b4 and the status dialog 0x65 are the renderer's; a target pool of −1 counts 0 records where the original reads the word before DGROUP:0x9406; the scrollbar re-lays itself out on a scale change; where the original stores an uninitialised local (the mode-1 flame of seg11:0x63b4) the port stores 0; a kept ship sheet's +0x5b and flame strip come from the last build's pixels, and the weapon images are built at every placement. [DESIGN]
- ADR 0001's open question on whether seg11:0x4db0 latches is answered: it does not; the pass rate did the work. [DESIGN] [BYTES seg11:0x4db0]
