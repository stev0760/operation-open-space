# ADR 0011: Audio

- **Status:** Accepted. [DESIGN]
- **Scope:** how the port plays the sound effects and the music that ISPACE.DLL plays through WAVEMIX.DLL, `sndPlaySound` and the MCI sequencer. The engine's rules stay in [sound.md](../re/sound.md); the sound catalogue is `re/artifacts/sounds.tsv`. [DESIGN]

## Context
- **The effects are 78 8-bit, 11 025 Hz, mono PCM WAVs** in `SND_FX.DLL` and `<Language>_FX.DLL`, addressed by slot id (resource `#100` id + 100). ISPACE.DLL plays them through WaveMix 1.70 when `WAVEMIX.DLL` is present and `MixChannels` is above 1, else through `sndPlaySound`. The WaveMix session is mono at the `WAVEMIX.INI` rate; every play clears the channel's queue at high priority; four ids loop 20, 20, 8 and 7 times. [BYTES ISPACE.DLL seg9:0x4e0 seg9:0xc42 seg9:0x11f4] [REF Microsoft WaveMix 1.x documentation]
- **Loudness and pan never touch the samples.** WaveMix has no per-channel volume, and ISPACE.DLL scales nothing: ordinal 8 computes left, right and overall volumes from the sound's position and the current priority sound sets them with one device-wide `waveOutSetVolume`; the music volume goes to `midiOutSetVolume` on device 1, which fails on modern systems and under Wine, so the music volume formula is never applied there. Under Wine 11 every `waveOutSetVolume` and `midiOutSetVolume` call of the original returns `MMSYSERR_INVALHANDLE`, so the oracle plays everything at full volume and centred. Focus loss and exit restore the system volumes they saved, and `ForceVol` forces those to maximum. [BYTES ISPACE.DLL seg9:0x76c seg9:0xa9e seg9:0x3906] [ORACLE start-0001] [ORACLE sound-0001]
- **The music is MIDI through MCI:** twelve tracks that are time sections of `MAIN.MID`, `RACE.MID`, `ANTHEM.MID` and `DEMON.MID`, opened as a `sequencer` device, seeked and played with `MCI_NOTIFY`, looped or chained from `MM_MCINOTIFY`, paused and resumed by the control codes and on focus. Windows 10 and 11 play MIDI through the Microsoft GS synth; Wine's `mciseq` needs a MIDI output that a bare prefix lacks. [BYTES ISPACE.DLL seg9:0x3906 seg9:0x3a48] [REF Win32 SDK, MCI sequencer]
- **In 1994 the music was FM.** On a Sound Blaster the Windows MIDI mapper sent the game's music to the card's OPL3 synth, so the sound the author heard while writing it is the FM rendering, not a wavetable. [DESIGN]
- **The user wants the original's sound and a way to hear it on Linux without configuring a synth.** [DESIGN]

## Decision
1. **One software mixer in the port replaces WaveMix and `sndPlaySound`.** It mixes the effects at their native 11 025 Hz, 8-bit, mono, with WaveMix's channel model: `MixChannels` channels (1 to 8), clear-queue high-priority plays, the loop counts, the busy-until holds, the priority ids, the stop and reset ids, and the single-channel semantics when `MixChannels` is 1 (`sndPlaySound`'s one voice, `SND_LOOP` for the looping ids). The mixed stream goes to `waveOut` (winmm) as 16-bit stereo. WaveMix's own summing and clipping arithmetic is [OPEN] in sound.md; until it is read, the mixer sums and clips. [DESIGN] [BYTES ISPACE.DLL seg9:0x11f4] [OPEN]
2. **Volume and pan apply in software, to the port's stream only.** The left, right and overall words that the original would hand to `waveOutSetVolume` scale the mixed effects, and the music volume formula scales the music, so the user hears what the author's volume model computes and the port never changes the system or device volume. `ForceVol` and the saved system volumes have nothing to act on and are read and logged. The capability level the port reports to its own settings code is 3 (left and right volume) whenever a `waveOut` device exists, so the Audio Options list offers every mode; there is no PC-speaker refusal. The mixer applies the volume words as linear amplitude; the `waveOutSetVolume` documentation lets a device read them logarithmically, so the taper the original's hardware applied is [OPEN]. [DESIGN] [BYTES ISPACE.DLL seg9:0x76c seg9:0xa9e seg9:0x22ca] [REF Win32 SDK, waveOutSetVolume] [OPEN]
3. **Music plays through one of three backends** behind one interface, chosen by the port-only key `[Port] Music` with the values `mci`, `soundfont` and `opl3`:
   - `mci` (the default on Windows): the original's MCI sequence, command for command, with the port's music volume applied through `midiOutSetVolume` on the MIDI mapper, where the original's device-1 call fails; the mapper's saved volume is restored on focus loss and at exit. Whether that call moves the per-application session volume on Windows Vista and later, which would also scale the port's effects stream, is [OPEN] until a run on real Windows; if it does, the music volume moves into software as decision 2 does for the effects. [DESIGN] [BYTES ISPACE.DLL seg9:0x3906] [OPEN]
   - `soundfont`: the port's own MIDI file player rendering through TinySoundFont (MIT, `third_party/tsf/`) with a General MIDI SoundFont the user names in `[Port] SoundFont`. With the key empty, the port looks for `Z:\usr\share\soundfonts\default.sf2` under Wine (Arch's `soundfont-fluid` package). An empty `[Port] Music` under Wine takes `opl3`, then `soundfont` when a SoundFont resolves, then `mci`, because Wine's MCI sequencer opens a "Midi Through" port with no synth behind it, so the MCI open never fails and the user would hear nothing; `opl3` comes first because the music was written on FM hardware; an explicit `mci` is always MCI. [DESIGN] [REF TinySoundFont] [ORACLE sound-0001]
   - `opl3`: the same player rendering through an OPL3 emulator with a Windows-era Sound Blaster FM bank, for the sound of the original hardware, built behind the CMake option `IS_OPL3`. libADLMIDI is ruled out: its core is GPL-3.0-or-later and the one bank imported from the Windows 3.x `msadlib.drv` is unlicensed. The backend is the Nuked OPL3 chip emulator alone (LGPL-2.1-or-later, `third_party/nuked-opl3/`, two files, linked statically) under a General MIDI voice driver written for the port (`opl3_driver.*`: 18 two-operator voices over both register banks, voice stealing by free, then longest released, then oldest, drums from the bank's percussion keys on channel 10, sustain, pitch bend with the RPN 0 range, CC 7/10/11/120/121/123, GM and GS reset), with the MIT-licensed Fat Man 2-operator set (`third_party/fatman-2op/`, the WOPL file embedded at configure time and parsed by `opl3_bank.*`) as the bank, which is the Windows 9x FM driver's patch set; the bank's deep tremolo and vibrato flags are written as the constant 0xBD = 0xC0. The Windows 3.x driver's own patches (the user's `msadlib.drv`) are not pursued: the 9x set is the one the game was mostly played with. The CMake option `IS_OPL3` is on by default and the presets set it, so an old cache picks it up on its next preset configure. Velocity, volume and expression follow a 40·log10 law in 0.75 dB TL steps (the GM2 and DLS curve) rather than the bank's Windows 9x volume model 4; how far that is from the 9x driver's loudness is [OPEN]. A released operator at full attenuation leaves a chip floor of a few LSB (about −68 dBFS) while the music is paused, as the chip does. Because the library is static, every binary release must carry the LGPL text and give access to the port's source (LGPL 2.1 section 6); the release packaging is [OPEN]. [DESIGN] [REF libADLMIDI v1.6.2.1 licence headers] [REF Nuked OPL3] [REF YMF262 data sheet] [OPEN]
   The two synth backends render into the port's mixer, so music and effects share one output stream, and the music volume formula scales their output in software (linear amplitude). The synth backend's end-of-play reaches the player as an `MM_MCINOTIFY` with `wParam` 1 and a sequence number in `lParam` (bit 31 set, where MCI's own `lParam` is a small device id), so one window procedure serves every backend. The track tables, section seeks, loops, chaining, control codes and the focus pause and resume are the same for every backend. The Midi Mapper question box is shown only by the `mci` backend, when MCI reports a port other than the mapper, as the original does. The game's four files are Microsoft dual-mode files (the sequencer meta `FF 7F 03 00 00 41`, with channels 13 to 16 a base-level copy of channels 1 to 10, see sound.md "Music"); both synth backends play channels 1 to 10 only, the extended level that the Windows 9x FM driver and a General MIDI synth are made for, because playing all sixteen doubles every part and sounds channel 16's drums as a piano; which level the author's 1994 set-up sounded is [OPEN] in sound.md; `mci` hands the file to the system sequencer untouched. The SoundFont synth's gain is −22 dB, the level at which the loudest sample of the four files stayed under full scale when all sixteen channels played (about 6 dB more would fit now); the OPL3 synth plays at the chip's unity gain (file peaks −7.4 to −9.2 dBFS, about 8.6 dB louder than the SoundFont by RMS, and the title mix with the loud effect bursts peaks within 0.1 dB of full scale). Both levels and the balance against the effects are [OPEN] until listened to. [DESIGN] [BYTES ISPACE.DLL seg9:0x3a80] [BYTES MAIN.MID] [OPEN]
4. **Implementation order:** the mixer and the effects first, then `mci`, then `soundfont`, then `opl3`. [DESIGN]
5. **Settings as the original:** the Audio Options and Options dialogs port with their control ids, fills, enable rules and INI writes; the device test reports no PC-speaker driver, so `AllowSound` never matters; `SoundSystem` −1 resolves to 3. The effect modules, the MIDI files and the custom WAVs (id −1) are read from `SndPath`; an empty `SndPath` keeps the game directory, where the original opens the module at the root of the current drive. Custom WAVs are read as any PCM WAV and converted to the mixer's format. The Options and Audio OK buttons write only their own keys, in the original's order. [DESIGN] [BYTES ISPACE.DLL seg9:0x2f30 seg9:0x15cc seg9:0xba seg9:0x162 seg9:0x20b3 seg9:0x33d6]
6. **Testing:** the mixer's channel model, holds, loops, variants and volume formulas are pure functions with doctest cases against the tables in sound.md; the catalogue test plays each id through the engine in a headless run and checks the log; music backends are checked by a render-to-file comparison of the first seconds of each track against a reference rendering kept under `work/`. [DESIGN]

## Alternatives
- **Shipping WAVEMIX.DLL semantics through a Win32 port of WaveMix:** the 1.x source is not available in a usable licence, and its behaviour is small enough to reproduce. [DESIGN]
- **`waveOutSetVolume` as the original:** on Windows Vista and later it sets the application's session volume, under Wine the device's; either way the user's mixer settings would move while playing. [DESIGN]
- **MCI only:** faithful and free of dependencies, but silent in a bare Wine prefix. [DESIGN]
- **A soft synth only:** drops the Windows user's GS synth, which is what the original uses there today. [DESIGN]

## Consequences
- M7 stage 1 implements decisions 1, 2, 4 (the mixer, the effects and `mci`) and 5 in `src/audio/` and `src/dialogs/audio_options.*`; stage 2 adds the MIDI file player (`midi_file.*`, `midi_player.*`), the synth contract (`synth.h`), `soundfont` (`music_soundfont.*`) and the backend choice (`music_select.*`); stage 2b adds `opl3` (`opl3_bank.*`, `opl3_driver.*`, `music_opl3.*`, `third_party/nuked-opl3/`, `third_party/fatman-2op/`) and the dual-mode channel rule in `midi_file.*`. [DESIGN]
- The section seek fast-forwards the file's program changes, controllers and pitch bends to the target and strikes no note, so a section that starts inside held notes opens on silence: tracks 4 and 11 open on 0.8 s and 1.8 s of silence and track 5's 0.8 s is silent. Whether the MCI sequencer sounds notes held across a seek target is [OPEN] in sound.md. [DESIGN] [OPEN]
- The port's contract is `src/audio/audio.h` (`init`, `shutdown`, `reset_slots`, `focus`, `reread_settings`, `play`, `play_at`, `frame`, `music`, `music_restart_stored`, `on_mci_notify`, `set_area`, `set_inspace_mix_channels`, `capability`, `dump_to`) and `src/audio/music.h` (`Backend`). A file that includes `audio.h` must not include `music.h` or `music_tracks.h`, because the function `is::audio::music` and the namespace `is::audio::music` cannot share one translation unit; `music_glue.*` bridges them. [DESIGN]
- `IS_AUDIO_DUMP=<path>` makes the output thread also write its stream to a WAV file, and counts one device when none exists, so headless runs can be compared by listening. [DESIGN]
- Checklist rows SND-01 to SND-13, WIN-28 and the INI rows for the sound keys describe the behaviour this ADR ports. [DESIGN]
- The port's sound log lines keep their ids; the catalogue gives each id its meaning and transcript. [DESIGN]
